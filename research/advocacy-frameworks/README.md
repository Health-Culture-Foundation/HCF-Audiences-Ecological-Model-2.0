# Advocacy-process frameworks for an HCF "advocacy phase" facet

Research date: 2026-10-22, by Claude (Claude Code) for HCF; not yet reviewed by a person. Companion data: [`frameworks.csv`](frameworks.csv) (one row per phase/step, with licence and verification columns).

## 1. GHAI Advocacy Action Guide (the one Kalman likes)

| Item | Finding |
|---|---|
| Title | *Advocacy Action Guide: Four Phases to Health Policy Success* |
| Publisher | Global Health Advocacy Incubator (GHAI), an initiative of the Campaign for Tobacco-Free Kids |
| Date | Copyright line says **2024**. The file is named `..._2023.pdf`; GHAI announced the revised guide on 2024-08-05; PDF metadata shows it was re-exported 2025-11-19. Best citation: GHAI, 2024. |
| Length | 52 pages (letter, landscape) |
| Lineage | Built on the 2019 Campaign for Tobacco-Free Kids / Consumers International *Advocacy Action Guide*; funded by Bloomberg Philanthropies |
| URL | https://assets.advocacyincubator.org/uploads/Advocacy_Action_Guide_2023.pdf (curl returned 200; no 403) |

**Licence statement (in the PDF, p. 2 and back cover).** It is not quite "all rights reserved." It reads, in short: the document "may be published, disseminated, shared, and used only with full attribution to GHAI" and "may not be changed, adapted, or altered in any way without consent from GHAI."

- In practice this works like CC BY-ND, though it isn't a CC licence. **HCF can link to the guide, host it, and share it unchanged with credit.** HCF cannot adapt, excerpt-and-edit, or rebuild its framework text without consent.
- The website copyright page (advocacyincubator.org/copyright) is stricter. It requires prior approval from GHAI and the Campaign for Tobacco-Free Kids to reproduce, distribute, or publish site materials. The PDF's own statement is the more specific one for the guide.
- **Permissions contact:** info@advocacyincubator.org, +1-202-296-5469, 1400 I Street NW, Suite 1200, Washington, DC 20005. (Media contact: Jen Patterson, jpatterson@advocacyincubator.org.)

**Structure (labels only):** 4 phases and 12 steps, drawn as a cycle.

| Phase | Steps | Phase outcome (paraphrased) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Analyze | 1 Conduct a landscape analysis · 2 Set your policy objective · 3 Assess the policy decision-making pathway | Political map |
| 2 Collaborate | 4 Build partnerships and champions · 5 Develop a strategic plan · 6 Plan communications | Shared strategy, with communications |
| 3 Advocate | 7 Influence decision-makers · 8 Build public support · 9 Counter opposition | Policy passed and put in place |
| 4 Sustain | 10 Monitor implementation · 11 Defend policy · 12 Refine campaign | Policy kept and improved |

The guide also has a Glossary and a Resources section.

**Related GHAI-authored guide:** *Advocacy Action Guide: Elements of a Policy Advocacy Campaign*. Resolve to Save Lives published it in 2024, but GHAI wrote it. It uses an older 13-element list, from "Gather background information" to "Celebrate success and remain engaged" (see CSV). The PDF has no licence statement, and the site footer says © Resolve to Save Lives. It isn't usable as an open base either.

**Licence takeaway:**
- **CDC (public domain) plus the academic policy cycle and Kingdon (concepts)** give a clean backbone for HCF's own facet.
- Single-word category names ("lobbying," "policy adoption") are ordinary vocabulary and are not protected. HCF should still **write its own labels and definitions**, and cite CEI/HFRP/GHAI as "informed by," not "adapted from." That way no ND or SA clause comes into play.
- Avoid copying definitions from WHO (CC BY-NC-SA). An adaptation would have to be released under the same licence and stay non-commercial.

## 3. Recommendation: HCF "Advocacy phase" facet (single facet)

Seven phases in plain language, in HCF's own wording, built on the CDC Policy Process, the policy cycle and Kingdon, with the CLM policy goals as a check. Two cross-cutting tags handle what CDC calls "overarching" work.

| # | HCF phase (proposed) | What a resource in this phase helps you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Size up the problem** | Gather data on who is harmed and how badly, and what laws exist now |
| 2 | **Pick the policy fix** | Compare options and choose a specific, winnable policy ask |
| 3 | **Plan the campaign** | Map who decides and how, set objectives, write the plan and messages |
| 4 | **Get it on the agenda** | Make decision-makers and the public see it as urgent now; use openings |
| 5 | **Win the decision** | Get the bill, rule, budget line or order passed or signed, or block a harmful one |
| 6 | **Put it into practice** | Make sure agencies fund, staff, enforce and explain the policy |
| 7 | **Protect and improve it** | Track results, defend against rollbacks, fix gaps, plan the next round |
| — | *Cross-cutting tag:* **Build support** | Partners, champions, public backing (CDC: stakeholder engagement; GHAI Phase 2/Step 8) |
| — | *Cross-cutting tag:* **Check what's working** | Evaluation and learning at any phase (CDC: evaluation) |

Decision (reversible): "Build support" is a cross-cutting tag, not a phase. GHAI makes it a phase ("Collaborate"), but CDC and CEI treat it as running through every phase, and a coalition-building guide would otherwise be tagged to 3–4 phases at once. If editors prefer it as a phase, insert it between 3 and 4. That gives 8 phases, still within the target range.

### Crosswalk

| HCF phase | CDC Policy Process (PD) | Policy cycle | Kingdon | HFRP CLM policy goals | GHAI 2024 steps (labels) | WHO/PMNCH adolescents | UNICEF 9 Qs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Size up the problem | Problem identification | (pre-agenda) | Problem stream | — | 1 Landscape analysis | Ch1 Get ready; Ch2 Gather info | Q1 (part) |
| 2 Pick the policy fix | Policy analysis | Formulation | Policy stream | Policy development | 2 Set policy objective | Ch4 (goal/objectives) | Q1 What do we want? |
| 3 Plan the campaign | Strategy & policy development | Formulation | — | — | 3 Decision-making pathway; 5 Strategic plan; 6 Plan communications | Ch4 Roadmap | Q2–Q8 |
| 4 Get it on the agenda | (Strategy; engagement) | Agenda setting | Politics stream; **policy window** | Placement on the agenda | 7 Influence decision-makers (early); 8 Public support | Ch3 Use global commitments | Q3–Q5 |
| 5 Win the decision | Policy enactment | Decision / adoption | Coupling at window | Adoption; **Blocking** | 7 Influence decision-makers; 9 Counter opposition | — | Q8 |
| 6 Put it into practice | Policy implementation | Implementation | — | Implementation | 10 Monitor implementation | Ch5 (monitor) | — |
| 7 Protect and improve it | Evaluation (overarching) | Evaluation (→ new agenda) | — | Monitoring & evaluation; **Maintenance** | 11 Defend policy; 12 Refine campaign | Ch5 Review, re-plan | Q9 |
| *Build support* (tag) | Stakeholder engagement & education | — | Policy entrepreneurs | (tactics: coalitions) | 4 Partnerships & champions; 8 Public support | Ch1 (partners) | Q4 |
| *Check what's working* (tag) | Evaluation | Evaluation | — | M&E | 12 Refine campaign | Ch4 M&E; Ch5 | Q9 |

GHAI lines up almost one-to-one: Analyze = 1–3, Collaborate = 3 + Build support, Advocate = 4–5, Sustain = 6–7. If GHAI later opens the guide up, HCF can show "GHAI step" as a secondary label without changing the facet.

### Phases and audiences

| HCF phase | Main target audience (who must change) | Who uses the resource |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Size up the problem | (none yet; research) | Advocates, researchers, data/epi staff |
| 2 Pick the policy fix | Policy experts, lawyers (as reviewers) | Advocates, coalition leads |
| 3 Plan the campaign | (internal) | Advocates, coalition partners |
| 4 Get it on the agenda | Journalists and media, community leaders, the public, policymakers' staff | Advocates, communicators |
| 5 Win the decision | **Policymakers** (legislators, executives, councils, boards); also the public/voters for ballot measures | Advocates, lobbyists, champions |
| 6 Put it into practice | **Regulators and agency staff**, enforcement officers, the businesses and services that must comply | Advocates, watchdogs, community monitors |
| 7 Protect and improve it | Policymakers (against rollback), courts (litigation), media | Advocates, legal partners, evaluators |
| Throughout | — | **Advocates** (coalitions, community groups, professional associations) use every phase |

## 4. Richer HCF advocacy framework (multiple independent axes)

Each axis is a separate facet, so one resource can carry tags on several axes at once. For example, a "testifying at a city council hearing" guide could be tagged *Win the decision* × *Testify and brief* × *Policymakers* × *Local* × *Legislature* × *Political will*.

| Axis | Values (plain language, HCF wording) | Built on | Licence of basis | HCF original wording? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Policy-process phase** | Size up the problem · Pick the policy fix · Plan the campaign · Get it on the agenda · Win the decision · Put it into practice · Protect and improve it | CDC Policy Process; policy cycle; Kingdon; checked against HFRP CLM and GHAI | Public domain + concepts | Yes: labels and definitions are new; structure is CDC's |
| **B. Advocacy activity (tactic)** | Research and analysis · Draft the policy (model laws, bill text) · Build coalitions · Organize communities · Train advocates · Educate decision-makers · Ask decision-makers directly (lobbying) · Testify and brief · Earned media and storytelling · Paid ads and campaigns · Digital and social outreach · Public events and rallies · Polling and public opinion · Pilots and demonstrations · Legal action · Monitor and enforce · Counter the opposition · Budget advocacy | Tactic lists in HFRP CLM and CEI ASF (©, reworded); CTB ch. 30–35 (licence conflict, reworded); GHAI step 9 and RTSL "monitor and report violations" (ideas only) | Mixed; only short generic terms used | Yes: all labels reworded and grouped by HCF. "Testify and brief," "Monitor and enforce," "Budget advocacy" are HCF additions. |
| **C. Target audience** | Policymakers (elected) · Regulators and agency staff · Courts and judges · Journalists and media · Community leaders · Health professionals and associations · Businesses and industry · The public (or a specific group) · Funders and donors · Other advocates and coalitions · Opponents (to anticipate) | Links to **HCF audience roles**. Grouping follows CEI's public / influencers / decision makers. CDC stakeholder engagement names decision-makers, partners, affected people, the public, and opponents. | ASF © (structure only); CDC PD | Yes: role names are HCF's taxonomy. Optional roll-up to CEI's three groups as "Public / Influencers / Decision-makers." |
| **D. Jurisdiction level** | Neighborhood/institution (school, hospital, employer) · City or county · Tribal nation · State or province · National/federal · Regional (multi-country, e.g. EU, AU) · Global (WHO, UN treaties) | Generic. CEI ASF footnote (local/state/federal); WHO/PMNCH ch. 3 on global commitments | n/a | Yes |
| **E. Decision venue (branch)** | Legislature or council · Executive or agency (rules, orders) · Budget process · Courts · Ballot measure · Institutional policy (board, workplace, school) · International body | CEI ASF footnote (legislative, executive, administrative, judicial); HFRP policy-goal examples (bills, ballot measures, regulations) | Concepts | Yes. Recommended addition: tactics differ more by venue than by level. |
| **F. Outcome sought (change in the audience)** | Three tiers, in HCF wording. **Know about it** (awareness). **Care about it** (salience, attitudes, public will, political will, framing). **Act on it** (new advocates, new champions, votes and decisions, support base grows). Plus a **Stronger movement** group: group capacity, partnerships, funding, visibility, media coverage. | CEI ASF changes (awareness/will/action); HFRP CLM interim outcomes | © (ideas only) | Yes: tier names "Know / Care / Act" and grouping are HCF's. Cite "informed by Coffman (HFRP 2009; CEI 2015)." |
| **G. Policy goal type** (optional) | Win a new policy · Block a harmful policy · Protect an existing policy · Win or protect funding · Get it enforced | HFRP CLM policy goals (adoption, blocking, maintenance, implementation) | © (ideas only) | Yes |
| **H. Theory of change** (optional, advanced) | Wait for or create a window · Big sudden shift · Coalition of shared beliefs · Win over power holders · Grassroots power · Reframe the message · Spread from early adopters | ORS Impact / CEI *Pathways for Change* (© all rights reserved) | © (ideas only) | Yes. Optional; probably only for funder and evaluator audiences. |

Notes on the axes:
- **A and G are separate on purpose.** A "defend against preemption" toolkit is phase *Protect and improve it* or *Win the decision*, with goal type *Block a harmful policy*. GHAI and CDC have no "block" concept; HFRP does.
- **C is where the socio-ecological model joins this framework.** At the public-policy level, audience roles are the people who make, carry out, interpret, or report on policy. Use the same role IDs as the rest of the HCF taxonomy; don't create advocacy-only copies.
- **B × A suggested defaults** (for editors, not enforced): Research → phases 1–2; Draft the policy → 2; Coalitions → Build support; Educate or ask decision-makers → 4–5; Media → 4–5; Legal action → 5, 7; Monitor and enforce → 6–7.
- **Open sources available per axis:** A is fully public domain/concept-based. B, F, G and H rest on copyrighted evaluation literature, used only for category ideas with HCF wording. C, D and E are HCF-original, lightly informed by public sources. Nothing needs a licence from CEI, HFRP or GHAI as long as HCF writes its own labels and definitions and doesn't reproduce their diagrams or text.

> **Subject:** Could the Advocacy Action Guide framework be openly licensed?
>
> Hi GHAI team,
>
> I'm Kalman, working with the Health Culture Foundation (HCF) on a free, searchable library of health-advocacy resources. Your *Advocacy Action Guide* (2024) is the clearest walk-through of the advocacy process we've found. The four phases and twelve steps match how advocates actually work.
