# HCF advocacy framework (v0.1)

_Generated by `scripts/build.py` from the CSV files in this folder. Draft; not reviewed by a person. Research and licence notes: [research/advocacy-frameworks](../../research/advocacy-frameworks/README.md)._

Use it two ways at once:

- **Simple:** tag a resource with one **phase** (axis A). That alone answers "where in the advocacy process does this help?"
- **Full:** add any of the other axes. Each axis is separate, so one resource can carry tags on several. A guide to testifying at a city council hearing: *Win the decision* × *Testify and brief* × *Decision-makers* × *City or county* × *Legislature or council* × *Political will*.

All labels and definitions are HCF's own wording. The phases are built on the public-domain CDC Policy Process, the policy cycle and Kingdon. Other axes borrow category ideas (not text) from copyrighted advocacy-evaluation work, credited per concept as "informed by". Full citations: [REFERENCES.md](../../REFERENCES.md) (CDC Policy Process, Lasswell 1956, Jann & Wegrich 2007, Kingdon 1984, Coffman 2009, Coffman & Beer 2015, Stachowiak 2013, GHAI 2024). GHAI's *Advocacy Action Guide* steps appear only as crosswalk labels ([`crosswalks/ghai-steps-to-advocacy-phases.sssom.tsv`](../../crosswalks/ghai-steps-to-advocacy-phases.sssom.tsv)); its terms allow sharing but not adaptation.

## A. Advocacy phase

Where in the policy process a resource helps: seven phases in HCF's own words, plus two tags that apply at any phase. Built on the CDC Policy Process (public domain), the policy cycle and Kingdon's multiple streams.

Source: [`phases.csv`](phases.csv)

| Value | Means | Links to audiences | Informed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Size up the problem** | Find out who is harmed, how badly, why, and which laws and policies exist now. _e.g. Mapping how many U.S. high schools start before 8:30 am, and which states set rules._ | `researchers`, `public-health-practitioners`, `policy-advocates` | CDC Policy Process: problem identification; Kingdon: problem stream; GHAI step 1 (label) |
| **Pick the policy fix** | Compare options and choose one specific, winnable policy to ask for. _e.g. Choosing an 8:30 am earliest-start rule over a later-bus-schedule pilot._ | `policy-advocates`, `researchers` | CDC Policy Process: policy analysis; policy cycle: formulation; Kingdon: policy stream; HFRP: policy development; GHAI step 2 (label) |
| **Plan the campaign** | Map who decides and how, set goals, and write the campaign and message plan. _e.g. Listing every vote needed on the state education committee._ | `policy-advocates`, `community-organizations` | CDC Policy Process: strategy and policy development; GHAI steps 3, 5, 6 (labels); UNICEF planning questions |
| **Get it on the agenda** | Make decision-makers and the public see the problem as urgent now, and use openings when they come. _e.g. Using the daylight-saving clock change to get coverage of permanent standard time._ | `journalists-and-media`, `community-and-religious-leaders`, `policymakers`, `the-person` | Policy cycle: agenda setting; Kingdon: politics stream and policy window; HFRP: placement on the agenda |
| **Win the decision** | Get the bill, rule, budget line or order passed or signed, or stop a harmful one. _e.g. Testifying at the hearing for a school start-time bill._ | `policymakers`, `regulators-and-agency-staff` | CDC Policy Process: enactment; policy cycle: decision; HFRP: adoption and blocking; GHAI steps 7, 9 (labels) |
| **Put it into practice** | Make sure agencies fund, staff, enforce and explain the policy, and that it reaches the people it was meant for. _e.g. Checking that districts actually changed bell times after the law took effect._ | `regulators-and-agency-staff`, `school-and-campus-leaders`, `employers-and-workplaces` | CDC Policy Process: implementation; policy cycle: implementation; HFRP: implementation; GHAI step 10 (label) |
| **Protect and improve it** | Track results, defend against rollbacks and loopholes, fix gaps, and plan the next round. _e.g. Fighting a bill that would let districts opt out of the start-time law._ | `policymakers`, `courts-and-judges`, `journalists-and-media` | Policy cycle: evaluation; HFRP: maintenance, monitoring and evaluation; GHAI steps 11, 12 (labels) |
| **Build support (any phase)** | Bring in partners, champions and public backing. Applies at every phase. _e.g. Recruiting pediatricians and coaches as start-time champions._ | `policy-advocates`, `community-organizations`, `clinicians` | CDC Policy Process: stakeholder engagement and education (overarching); GHAI phase 'Collaborate' and steps 4, 8 (labels) |
| **Check what's working (any phase)** | Measure progress and learn, at any phase. _e.g. A mid-campaign poll of parent support._ | `researchers`, `funders` | CDC Policy Process: evaluation (overarching) |

## B. Advocacy tactic

What the advocate does. HCF wording; category ideas from advocacy-evaluation literature (HFRP Composite Logic Model, CEI Advocacy Strategy Framework), which is not copied.

Source: [`tactics.csv`](tactics.csv)

| Value | Means | Links to audiences | Informed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Research and analysis** | Gather and analyze evidence to make the case. |  | HFRP CLM tactics; CTB ch. 31 (ideas only) |
| **Draft the policy** | Write the actual policy, bill, rule or model law. |  | HFRP CLM: policy proposal development (idea) |
| **Build coalitions** | Bring organizations together behind one ask. |  | HFRP CLM; CEI ASF (ideas only) |
| **Organize communities** | Help affected people organize and act for themselves. |  | HFRP CLM: grassroots organizing (idea) |
| **Train advocates** | Teach people and groups how to advocate. |  | HCF addition; GHAI Capacity Strengthening program (idea) |
| **Educate decision-makers** | Give decision-makers and their staff information, without a specific ask. |  | HFRP CLM: policymaker education (idea) |
| **Ask decision-makers directly** | Ask a decision-maker to support or oppose a specific policy. |  | HFRP CLM: lobbying (idea) |
| **Testify and brief** | Speak or submit comments in a formal hearing or consultation. |  | HCF addition |
| **Earned media and storytelling** | Get coverage and tell stories that make the issue real. |  | HFRP CLM: earned media (idea) |
| **Paid ads and campaigns** | Pay for messages to reach the public or decision-makers. |  | HFRP CLM: paid media, PSAs (idea) |
| **Digital and social outreach** | Reach and mobilize people online. |  | HFRP CLM: digital and social media (idea) |
| **Public events and rallies** | Gather people in public to show support. |  | HFRP CLM: rallies and marches (idea) |
| **Polling and public opinion** | Measure what the public and voters think. |  | HFRP CLM: polling (idea) |
| **Pilots and demonstrations** | Show the policy working on a small scale first. |  | HFRP CLM: demonstration projects (idea) |
| **Legal action** | Use the courts to win, enforce or defend a policy. | `courts-and-judges` | HFRP CLM: litigation (idea) |
| **Monitor and enforce** | Check that a policy is followed and report when it isn't. |  | HCF addition; GHAI step 10 and RTSL 'monitor and report violations' (ideas only) |
| **Counter the opposition** | Anticipate and answer the arguments and tactics of opponents. |  | GHAI step 9 (label); CTB ch. 35 (idea) |
| **Budget advocacy** | Win, protect or track public money for the policy. |  | HCF addition |

## F. Outcome sought

Source: [`outcomes.csv`](outcomes.csv)

|---|---|---|---|
| **Know about it** | The audience learns the problem exists and what could fix it. |  | Informed by CEI ASF (awareness) and HFRP CLM interim outcomes; HCF wording |
| **Care about it** | The audience sees it as important and wants to act. |  | Informed by CEI ASF (will); HCF wording |

## H. Theory of change

Optional, for funders and evaluators. How the campaign expects change to happen.

Source: [`theory-of-change.csv`](theory-of-change.csv)

| Value | Means | Links to audiences | Informed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wait for or create a window** | Change comes when problem, solution and politics line up. |  | Kingdon (concept); Pathways for Change (idea, all rights reserved) |
| **Big sudden shift** | Change comes in rare big jumps after long stability. |  | Baumgartner & Jones (concept) |
| **Coalition of shared beliefs** | Change comes when groups who share core beliefs work together. |  | Sabatier advocacy coalition framework (concept) |
| **Win over power holders** | Change comes by persuading the few people with power. |  | Pathways for Change (idea) |
| **Grassroots power** | Change comes when affected people build power together. |  | Pathways for Change (idea) |
| **Reframe the message** | Change comes when the issue is seen in a new way. |  | Pathways for Change (idea) |
| **Spread from early adopters** | Change spreads from places that try it first. _e.g. California's start-time law cited by other states._ |  | Rogers diffusion of innovations (concept) |
