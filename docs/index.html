Audiences by ecological level

A shared way to tag who a health resource is for, and who acts on it, using the five levels of influence of the socio-ecological model. Any library, glossary or website can use it. Sleep is the first worked example.

Levels from McLeroy et al. (1988) [McLeroy et al. 1988], as presented in NCI's Theory at a Glance (2005), Table 1 [NCI 2005]. Crosswalked to CDC's four-level model [CDC SEM].

The five levels

Level 1 · Intrapersonal level

The person

What one person knows, believes, feels and is able to do, and the traits they bring to a choice.

Sleep example: A teen reads why phones keep them awake and decides to charge theirs outside the bedroom.

NCI (2005): “Individual characteristics that influence behavior, such as knowledge, attitudes, beliefs, and personality traits”

22 audiences · CDC: Individual

Level 2 · Interpersonal level

Close relationships

The people closest to someone (family, partners, friends, peers, caregivers) and how they support, pressure or model a behavior.

Sleep example: A parent sets a family rule that all screens go in the kitchen at 9 pm.

NCI (2005): “Interpersonal processes and primary groups, including family, friends, and peers that provide social identity, support, and role definition”

11 audiences · CDC: Relationship

Level 3 · Institutional or organizational level

Organizations

The rules, schedules and habits of the organizations where people spend their time: schools, workplaces, clinics, colleges, the military, care homes.

Sleep example: A school district moves the high-school start time to 8:30 am.

NCI (2005): “Rules, regulations, policies, and informal structures, which may constrain or promote recommended behaviors”

11 audiences · CDC: Community

Level 4 · Community level

Community

The networks and shared norms that connect people, groups and organizations in a place or a shared identity: neighborhoods, faith groups, coalitions, local media, online communities.

Sleep example: A city coalition runs a campaign so that 'teens need more sleep' becomes common sense among parents and coaches.

NCI (2005): “Social networks and norms, or standards, which exist as formal or informal among individuals, groups, and organizations”

11 audiences · CDC: Community

Level 5 · Public policy level

Policy

Laws, regulations, funding and public policies at every level of government, from a town council to national governments and international bodies.

Sleep example: A state passes a law setting the earliest start time for public high schools.

NCI (2005): “Local, state, and federal policies and laws that regulate or support healthy actions and practices for disease prevention, early detection, control, and management”

7 audiences · CDC: Societal

Three rules

1. An audience sits at the level where it acts.
A parent reading about a teen's sleep is level 2. A principal changing the bell schedule is level 3. A legislator writing a start-time bill is level 5.
2. Who it's for ≠ who it's about.
Roles (parent, school leader, policymaker) say who uses a resource. Populations (teens, shift workers) say whose health it's about. Tag both.
3. Crosswalks, not merges.
Each library keeps its own tags and maps them here with SSSOM files [Matentzoglu et al. 2022], using SKOS [SKOS] and MeSH [MeSH] as the hub.
