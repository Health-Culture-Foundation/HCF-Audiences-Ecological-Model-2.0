Five levels of influence (McLeroy et al. 1988) to the CDC four-level social-ecological model

CDC publishes no URIs for its four levels, so cdcsem IDs are minted by HCF (see vocab/cdc-sem-levels.ttl). Direction is five-level -> four-level. Reverse lookups (four -> five) are one-to-many for community and societal.

closeMatchcdcsem:individual
Individual
Same idea; CDC also places biology and personal history (age, income, history of abuse) here, which NCI's definition does not mention.
hcflvl:interpersonal
Interpersonal level
closeMatchcdcsem:relationship
Relationship
Both name family, partners/friends and peers. NCI treats a clinician in a one-to-one visit as interpersonal; CDC is silent.
hcflvl:institutional
Institutional or organizational level
broadMatchcdcsem:community
Community
CDC's community level names schools and workplaces as settings, so organizations sit inside it. The CDC model has no separate organizational level.
hcflvl:community
Community level
broadMatchcdcsem:community
Community
CDC community = settings (schools, workplaces, neighborhoods); NCI community = networks and norms among groups and organizations. Overlap on neighborhoods and networks.
hcflvl:community
Community level
relatedMatchcdcsem:societal
Societal
Social and cultural norms are 'community' in NCI's table but 'societal' in CDC's. Norms content may need both tags.
hcflvl:public-policy
Public policy level
broadMatchcdcsem:societal
Societal
CDC societal includes health, economic, educational and social policies plus cultural norms; public policy is the narrower piece.
