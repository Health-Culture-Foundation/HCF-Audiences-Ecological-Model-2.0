Advocacy framework

For resources at the public-policy level: where in the advocacy process does this help, and how?

Simple: tag one phase.
  1. Size up the problem
  2. Pick the policy fix
  3. Plan the campaign
  4. Get it on the agenda
  5. Win the decision
  6. Put it into practice
  7. Protect and improve it

Plus two tags for work at any phase: Build support (any phase), Check what's working (any phase).

Full: add any other axis. Each axis is separate, so one resource can carry several tags.

A guide to testifying at a city council hearing: Win the decision × Testify and brief × Decision-makers × City or county × Legislature or council × Political will.

Axes: A Advocacy phase · B Tactic · C Target · D Jurisdiction · E Decision venue · F Outcome sought · G Policy goal type · H Theory of change

How the phases line up with other frameworks

The phases are written in HCF's own words on top of CDC's public-domain Policy Process [CDC Policy Process], the policy cycle [Lasswell 1956] [Jann & Wegrich 2007] and Kingdon's multiple streams [Kingdon 1984], checked against the Composite Logic Model's policy goals [Coffman 2009]. GHAI's Advocacy Action Guide [GHAI 2024] may be shared but not adapted, so its step names appear here only as matching labels.

The axes

All labels and definitions are HCF's wording. "Informed by" names where a category idea came from; no text is copied.

A. Advocacy phase

ValueMeansLinks to audiencesInformed by
Size up the problem
problem identification, landscape analysis, needs assessment
Find out who is harmed, how badly, why, and which laws and policies exist now.
e.g. Mapping how many U.S. high schools start before 8:30 am, and which states set rules.
researchers, public-health-practitioners, policy-advocatesCDC Policy Process: problem identification; Kingdon: problem stream; GHAI step 1 (label)
Pick the policy fix
policy analysis, policy objective, policy options
Compare options and choose one specific, winnable policy to ask for.
e.g. Choosing an 8:30 am earliest-start rule over a later-bus-schedule pilot.
policy-advocates, researchersCDC Policy Process: policy analysis; policy cycle: formulation; Kingdon: policy stream; HFRP: policy development; GHAI step 2 (label)
Plan the campaign
strategy, campaign plan, power mapping, decision-making pathway
Map who decides and how, set goals, and write the campaign and message plan.
e.g. Listing every vote needed on the state education committee.
policy-advocates, community-organizationsCDC Policy Process: strategy and policy development; GHAI steps 3, 5, 6 (labels); UNICEF planning questions
Get it on the agenda
agenda setting, policy window, raise the issue
Make decision-makers and the public see the problem as urgent now, and use openings when they come.
e.g. Using the daylight-saving clock change to get coverage of permanent standard time.
journalists-and-media, community-and-religious-leaders, policymakers, the-personPolicy cycle: agenda setting; Kingdon: politics stream and policy window; HFRP: placement on the agenda
Win the decision
policy enactment, adoption, passage, block a harmful policy
Get the bill, rule, budget line or order passed or signed, or stop a harmful one.
e.g. Testifying at the hearing for a school start-time bill.
Use goal type 'Block a harmful policy' for defensive campaigns.
policymakers, regulators-and-agency-staffCDC Policy Process: enactment; policy cycle: decision; HFRP: adoption and blocking; GHAI steps 7, 9 (labels)
Put it into practice
policy implementation, enforcement, rollout
Make sure agencies fund, staff, enforce and explain the policy, and that it reaches the people it was meant for.
e.g. Checking that districts actually changed bell times after the law took effect.
regulators-and-agency-staff, school-and-campus-leaders, employers-and-workplacesCDC Policy Process: implementation; policy cycle: implementation; HFRP: implementation; GHAI step 10 (label)
Protect and improve it
sustain, defend, maintenance, evaluation, refine
Track results, defend against rollbacks and loopholes, fix gaps, and plan the next round.
e.g. Fighting a bill that would let districts opt out of the start-time law.
policymakers, courts-and-judges, journalists-and-mediaPolicy cycle: evaluation; HFRP: maintenance, monitoring and evaluation; GHAI steps 11, 12 (labels)
Build support (any phase)
partnerships, coalitions, champions, stakeholder engagement
Bring in partners, champions and public backing. Applies at every phase.
e.g. Recruiting pediatricians and coaches as start-time champions.
Cross-cutting tag, not a phase in sequence. Open question: make it phase 3.5 (8 phases)?
policy-advocates, community-organizations, cliniciansCDC Policy Process: stakeholder engagement and education (overarching); GHAI phase 'Collaborate' and steps 4, 8 (labels)
Check what's working (any phase)
evaluation, learning, monitoring
Measure progress and learn, at any phase.
e.g. A mid-campaign poll of parent support.
Cross-cutting tag, not a phase in sequence.
researchers, fundersCDC Policy Process: evaluation (overarching)

H. Theory of change optional

ValueMeansLinks to audiencesInformed by
Wait for or create a window
policy window, focusing event
Change comes when problem, solution and politics line up.Kingdon (concept); Pathways for Change (idea, all rights reserved)
Big sudden shift
large leaps, punctuated equilibrium
Change comes in rare big jumps after long stability.Baumgartner & Jones (concept)
Coalition of shared beliefs
advocacy coalition
Change comes when groups who share core beliefs work together.Sabatier advocacy coalition framework (concept)
Win over power holders
power politics, elite influence
Change comes by persuading the few people with power.Pathways for Change (idea)
Grassroots power
community organizing
Change comes when affected people build power together.Pathways for Change (idea)
Reframe the message
messaging and frameworks
Change comes when the issue is seen in a new way.Pathways for Change (idea)
Spread from early adopters
diffusion, policy diffusion
Change spreads from places that try it first.
e.g. California's start-time law cited by other states.
Rogers diffusion of innovations (concept)
